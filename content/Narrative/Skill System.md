
### Professions
**Professions** represent areas of skill and knowledge tied to specific subjects familiar to the character. These are player-defined titles that fit their character, within reasonable limitations. If a profession exists within a hierarchy, it is named after the lowest rank (e.g., both a fresh recruit and a general share the Profession **Soldier**, with distinctions reflected in their [[Bond]]).

>[!Example] List of Professions
>Farmer, Soldier, Mercenary, Assassin, Wizard, Alchemist, Priest, Merchant, Barbarian, Nomad, Smith, Scholar, Fisherman, Chef.

**Professions** encompass knowledge, skills, and social interactions associated with the title. 
Unless otherwise stated, they apply only to out-of-combat situations and do not govern mechanical abilities (e.g., Profession **Wizard** does not grant spellcasting). They also do not replace [[Bond]]. **Professions** get subtracted from the difficulty of the dice roll
>[!Example]
>Bobby the Thief with **Grace** **3** is trying a **Difficult Roll** (20) for a picking a lock, he has **Thief** **3**, so he rolls against a Grace Thief Difficult Roll = **20** - **3** (Grace) - **3** (Thief) = **14**

>[!Example]- Example
> : A Soldier trying to bond with a veteran of war to get information rolls Soldier (Soul), A chef adjusting a recipe for an important client rolls Chef (Grace), A Thief trying to lockpick a door rolls Thief (Grace)
### Culture
**Culture** functions similarly to Professions but is derived from the character’s cultural background. **Culture** is typically represented by two words: the **Race** and the **Location** of their cultural origin (e.g., Elf - Rosario). Characters can belong to a culture of a different race than their own, which can be explored during gameplay (e.g., an Orc raised by Elves).

The specificity of a Culture depends on the campaign’s scale. For instance, if a campaign is set in France, specifying an exact village in Italy may be less useful than simply noting “Italy.” 
### Hobby
**Hobbies** represent activities or areas of knowledge in which a character has some skill but lacks the depth of expertise of a professional. Unlike Professions, which are written as titles, Hobbies are expressed as verbs or specific areas of knowledge.
>[!Example] List of Hobbies
> Cooking, Dancing, Painting, Horse-riding, Climbing, Philosophy, Escaping, Lockpicking, Linguistics, Gambling, Disguise, Anatomy, Animal Handling, Driving.

A **Hobby** differs from a Profession by being more narrowly focused and limited to surface-level knowledge or the activity itself. Hobbies can also intersect with **Culture** if a character has spent significant time in a particular region (e.g., during a military campaign). In such cases, the character might adopt that Culture or its language as a Hobby.

>[!Example]- Difference between Profession and Hobby
A **Smith** (Profession) knows how to smith, understands the rules of local guilds, knows typical earnings for smiths, where to source quality materials, and how to spot scams.  
A **Smithing** (Hobby) knows how to smith but lacks deeper knowledge. The character might bond with professional smiths as a hobbyist speaking to an expert. While they could identify low-quality products, they wouldn’t know if a high price is standard in the city.
